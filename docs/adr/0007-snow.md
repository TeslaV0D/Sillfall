# ADR-0007: Snow

- Status: accepted (visuals subject to maintainer sign-off)
- Date: 2026-11-11
- Milestone: M5

## Context

PLAN.md, section 4.2 describes snow:

- Flakes in several variants,
- a height field per surface with repose angle,
- shedding on close, shake, and acceleration,
- melting.

Some points were open in the plan or did not fit the existing renderer. Decisions are recorded here.

## Decisions

### 1. Flakes as a dedicated sprite shape instead of a texture atlas

The plan mentions a sprite atlas with 4–6 procedural variants. Instead the sprite shader draws a new `Flake` shape directly from a distance function. Six variants: soft blob, star, branched star, plate, broad star, and cluster. Variant lives in the unused padding of the 48-byte sprite (`Sprite.Variant`).

Rationale: No texture, no asset, no license question, no second draw call. Shapes stay sharp at any scale. Small flakes (radius under 2 px × DPI) are always blobs because a star would not read at that size.

### 2. Depth

As with rain, flakes have depth. 65% are close enough to land on windows. The rest fall behind windows and are not drawn there. There is no separate setting like rain depth; the plan does not define one for snow.

### 3. Height field

`SnowField` stores height in pixels per bin (2 px × DPI) and volume in px² so volume is preserved on resample.

- **Repose angle:** Adjacent bins differ by at most one bin width (~45°). Two passes per step with alternating direction suffice.
- **Edges:** Nothing beyond the edge to lean on. Snow above allowed edge height falls off the end and is shed as a clump.
- **Max height 28 px × DPI:** A full bin gives snow to neighbors up to 8 bins away. Only what finds no room falls off the nearer end. Landing clumps do not spike, and nothing is lost on the ground.
- **Mass conservation:** Bookkeeping is tested: volume on field + shed + melted = added. Holds after thousands of random ops, including resize. Closing a window is tested: its snow lands entirely on the ground.

### 4. Ground

The top of the work area (usually the taskbar) also has a height field. Snow that slips off monitor edges disappears.

### 5. Shedding

- **Close, minimize, hide:** All snow breaks into clumps ~7 px × DPI wide.
- **Shake:** like close; clumps are thrown up and sideways.
- **Jerk:** If a window changes speed between moves by more than 2500 px/s × DPI, it loses some snow. Also applies when stopping abruptly from fast motion. Snow keeps the old velocity. Slow drags carry snow along.
- **Clumps:** Never land on the window they came from. They pass its top edge first and deposit where they land: on lower windows or the ground.

The jerk threshold is estimated; real mouse motion measurements are still missing. `WindowMotionTracker` computes the first move out of rest over the whole rest period, so only a mid-move jerk or hard stop counts. Sign-off must confirm.

### 6. Melting

When it stops snowing, snow melts. Each height loses 8% per second plus 0.15 px × DPI. A full pile is gone in ~35 seconds. Rain speeds that up (PLAN.md, section 4.7), including while it still snows. Below 0.3 px × DPI an edge counts as clear so residue does not keep the render loop alive. Dripping melt water (optional in plan) is not implemented. Coupling to temperature from weather sync follows with M10.

### 7. Drawing piles

The plan mentions a triangle strip with SDF edge. Implemented: one column of rectangle sprites per bin:

- whole pixels opaque,
- fractional part as a semi-transparent top row for a soft edge,
- light blue shadow in the lower part,
- occasional glitter stars.

No new pipeline; at most three sprites per bin. If sign-off wants a softer contour, a dedicated strip pass is next.

## Consequences

- Sandbox is the shedding demo:
  - `--effect=snow --fill-snow` or keys W and F,
  - then shake, fling, or close windows with Delete.
- Smoke test checks snow on the runner: render loop and stop after melt. Screenshot compares with and without snow.
